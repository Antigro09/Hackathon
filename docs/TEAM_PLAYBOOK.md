## 3. The script

### Ricardo (Business lead): hook (0:00–0:20)
> "A recurring sound can be hard to explain. Loudoun County describes particular challenges in measuring and regulating tonal and low-frequency noise. **Residents need a clear way to document what they notice.** Tate has the county's context."

**Ricardo's delivery:**
- Look at the judges and speak slowly.
- Pause after "hard to explain."
- Turn toward Tate as you say his name.

### Tate: county context (0:20–0:40)
*(Tate may read this paraphrase from a card. It is not a resident quotation.)*
> "The county describes persistent hum or buzzing among the sounds associated with data centers. It also explains why A-weighted measurements alone may not represent low-frequency and tonal concerns well. **That gives us a reason to look carefully, not a license to identify a source.**"

**Tate's delivery:**
- Hold the card at chest height so your face stays visible.
- Look up between sentences.
- On the last sentence, look back toward Ricardo.

### Ricardo: the resident's task (0:40–1:00)
> "One short recording can't tell us everything. A person may hear a recurring sound at particular times, and different devices can capture it differently. **We bring the sample, the person's notes, and the limits together** so they can explain an observation and ask for an appropriate review."

### Vihaan (Technical lead): the science (1:00–1:50)
*(Vihaan holds a phone at waist level. It is a prop, not a second measurement device.)*
> "**We apply two frequency weightings to the same digital recording.** A-weighting reduces low-frequency contributions more than C-weighting. *(Gesture 1.)* A larger C-minus-A difference can suggest more bass contribution in the captured spectrum. *(Gesture 2.)*
>
> **A separate check looks for a narrow peak that persists through the sample.** That doesn't tell us whether it came from machinery, traffic, or something else.
>
> This runs in a compatible browser with microphone permission. *(Gesture 3.)* **These are uncalibrated digital results, not certified sound-pressure readings or a legal test.** The useful output is an observation with context and references. Anthony, show them."

**Vihaan's three gestures:**

| # | Starts on the words | What your hands do |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | Between gestures | Hold the phone at waist level, screen toward you. |
| 1 | "reduces low-frequency contributions" | Lower one hand slightly to illustrate de-emphasis. |
| 2 | "larger C-minus-A difference" | Separate your hands slightly to illustrate a difference. Neither curve is being presented as a flat response. |
| 3 | "compatible browser" | Briefly show the phone, then lower it. Do not claim every phone has a suitable microphone. |

**Vihaan's delivery:**
- Say each sentence, then pause.
- Make "separate check" clear: a large gap does not establish a tone.
- Keep the limit in the explanation. Do not promise source identification or that a county will order a measurement.

## 4. Why these words work

- Start with the resident's task, then show what the app contributes.
- Separate the **screening result** from the person's experience and any suspected source.
- Tell judges where to look using labels that actually appear in the app.
- Keep limits in the explanation, instead of trying to repair an overclaim during questions.
- End with a useful outcome: an observation another person can understand.

**Plain words to use on stage:**

| Technical term | Plain explanation |
|---|---|
| A-weighting | "a weighting that reduces low-frequency contributions" |
| C-weighting | "a weighting that retains more low-frequency contribution relative to A" |
| C−A | "the captured-spectrum gap"; not perceived loudness |
| Digital level | "a level relative to a digital reference, not calibrated sound pressure" |
| Sustained-peak candidate | "a narrow feature persisting through this sample"; not a machine identification |
| Spectrum | "how recorded energy is distributed across frequencies" |
| Inconclusive | "this sample doesn't support a usable indicator"; not proof nothing was heard |

## 5. Speaking techniques

**Voice**
- Slow down and project toward the furthest judge.
- Stress the bold words; pause between ideas.
- Finish sentences clearly. Match your certainty to the evidence.

**Pauses**
- Replace filler words with a short pause.
- Give judges time to read a displayed result.
- Stay silent during capture, including the warm-up.

**Eyes and body**
- Look at one judge for a sentence, then another.
- Stand still without blocking the screen.
- Look at teammates during their parts; avoid side conversations.

**Handoffs**
- Say the next person's name and turn toward them.
- If someone loses their place, use the next clear handoff rather than guessing a technical claim.

**Memorizing**
- Know your first sentence, last sentence, and handoff.
- Keep the middle natural. Tate may use the context card.

## 6. Judge questions

**Answer in two sentences when possible.** A person asked by name answers first; technical follow-ups go to Vihaan. If something is unverified, say so.

**Everyone's one-sentence answer:**
> "TonalWatch turns a live microphone sample and a person's notes into a limited, source-linked noise observation."

| Likely question | Owner | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| How accurate is a phone mic? | Vihaan | We have not established field accuracy across devices. Frequency response, processing, placement, and clipping can change the captured spectrum, so this is uncalibrated screening. |
| How does the analysis work? | Vihaan | It averages spectral power from overlapping FFT windows, then applies A and C weightings to the same samples. A separate experimental heuristic checks whether a narrow peak persists; it does not separate machinery from traffic or other sources. |
| Why 15 dB as the line? | Vihaan | It is a prototype screening convention, clearly labeled in the app. It is not an ordinance limit, a universal diagnostic cutoff, or a proof of a tone. |
| Why are some numbers negative? | Vihaan | The A and C values use RMS 1 as a digital reference, so a smaller digital signal can have a negative level. C−A is a separate difference and can also be negative; neither value is calibrated sound pressure. |
| Can I upload a recording or analyze a generated signal? | Anthony | Observation inputs are live microphone captures only, for 5, 15, or 30 seconds. Learn has explicitly played listening tones, but those do not create observation results. |
| What does the AI do? | Vihaan | The current optional hints compare the first five seconds with eight fixed text descriptions using a local pretrained model. The relative scores are not probabilities or confirmed sound types, and hints are excluded from reports. |
| Did your new training make it reliable? | Vihaan | Research heads and their recorded evaluations are separate from the live hints. We must pass the fixed data, rejection, browser-parity, and licensing gates before integrating a candidate; public-data results alone do not establish field reliability. |
| Why not run AI during this pitch? | Anthony | The first explicit Explore action downloads model files and runtime assets and can take time. The ordinary spectrum, indicators, and report work independently. |
| Is this proof against a data center? | Ricardo | No: neither a peak nor a gap establishes a source or a violation. The report records context for review; it cannot guarantee an investigation or a professional measurement. |
| Does anything leave the device? | Anthony | Audio is processed locally and isn't uploaded or attached to the report. Optional model downloads, external links, and explicitly loaded street tiles make network requests; users decide whether to share their exported notes and report. |
| How are you different from other tools? | Ricardo | Our focus is the combined observation workflow: spectrum, separate screens, context, quality notes, and references. We are not claiming that no competitor offers similar features. |
| How would you reach users or sustain it? | Ricardo | The proposed first step is one willing HOA pilot, with feedback from residents and qualified reviewers. Any broader distribution or paid service is a hypothesis, not established demand or revenue. |
| What's on the map? | Anthony | Loudoun County GIS data-center buildings, news-reported noise areas, OSM data-center tags after an explicit search, and public observations that contributors chose to publish. Those observations are self-reported and unverified, with rounded locations. There is no automatic GPS collection. |
| Can someone fake or spoil a reading? | Anthony | Live microphone input can still be contaminated or deliberately manipulated. Quality flags catch some problems, not every bad recording, and the app cannot authenticate a physical source. |
| What did you build versus AI assistance? | Anthony | State the team's actual use of coding agents and each person's actual decisions and verification work. Do not invent authorship or claim a feature was validated because an agent generated it. |
| Why is this an environmental concern? | Tate | Persistent neighborhood noise is part of the living environment. Loudoun's guidance discusses community concerns and acknowledges that more research is needed on long-term low-frequency effects; this app makes no health diagnosis. |
| Did you start before today? | Vihaan | Describe the actual timeline and ask the organizers how their rules apply. Commit timestamps alone do not settle eligibility or establish when all work began. |

## 7. Sources and boundaries

**Reviewed October 18, 2026. Recheck current county guidance before presenting legal details.** These sources provide context; a county profile does not determine property-specific applicability.

- **Loudoun County, Noise & Air Quality Concerns:** describes low-frequency/tonal measurement challenges, multiple measurement approaches, and planned amendments anticipated in spring or summer 2027. That is an expectation, not a guaranteed hearing or enactment date. A-weighting de-emphasizes bass; it does not make bass physically unmeasurable. https://www.loudoun.gov/6405/Noise-Air-Quality-Concerns
- **Prince William County, Steady Tonal Noise:** its current guidance points to Chapter 14 as modified effective May 1, 2026 and requires Type 1 meters capable of A/C and one-third-octave measurements for official inspections. Check the full code, exceptions, and agency instructions; this app does not implement that legal procedure. https://pwcaws.pwcva.gov/department/public-works/steady-tonal-noise
- The earlier NBC4 phone-app decibel figure, unsourced resident quotations, competitor comparisons, viral-adoption statistics, and hearing-protection inference are omitted from this script. They are not needed to demonstrate the product and were not independently established here.
- Check current AI status against the app's **Model, preprocessing & limitations** disclosure. The five-class research experiments are not the live eight-description comparison and must not be advertised as its accuracy.

**Describe the current app accurately:**
- Live microphone input only, with 5, 15, or 30-second capture choices. FFT size remains 8,192 samples; a longer capture adds frames, not finer FFT bin spacing.
- Captured-spectrum C−A and a separate experimental sustained-peak screen. A large difference does not establish a tone, and a peak does not identify a source.
- Uncalibrated weighted digital levels, not dBA/dBC sound-pressure measurements. Constant gain can cancel in C−A on otherwise identical unclipped samples; device filtering and processing do not.
- A printable observation packet and a shorter text summary. No automatic complaint filing, audio upload, or calibrated compliance result.
- Optional uncertain AI text matches from the first five seconds, excluded from the report. Research training and browser qualification remain separate from production claims.
- A community map of self-reported, unverified public observations that contributors choose to publish, with rounded locations. County GIS and OSM data-center layers are building records, not noise readings or sources.

**Never substitute these claims:**
- ~~"The law can't hear it" / "the app proves a violation."~~ Say that qualified measurements and agency review are needed.
- ~~"It found the exact pitch of the machine."~~ Say "a candidate near the displayed frequency," only if the result actually shows one.
- ~~"Not indicated means this room is quiet."~~ The screen describes this captured spectrum, not absolute environmental quietness or a person's experience.
- ~~"It's probably the ventilation."~~ Do not assign a source from these indicators or AI rankings.
- ~~"A phone flag is always conservative."~~ Unknown device response can change results in different directions.
- ~~"Our trained model is deployed and field accurate."~~ Report the actual release status and separate experimental results.
- ~~"Any phone works" / "nothing leaves the device."~~ Use the compatible-browser and network distinctions above.

## 8. How to practice

- Read your part aloud and time it against the speaking-order table.
- Run the real microphone sequence on the presentation laptop, including permission, visible-tab recording, and a permission-denied or canceled attempt.
- Check every spoken UI label against the app. Rehearse the honest failure branch without relying on removed controls.
- Practice explaining a suggested, below-threshold, and inconclusive indicator without inventing a physical cause.
- Use the actual displayed values during rehearsal; don't memorize a particular gap or peak.
- Practice the AI answer without calling scores probabilities or promising a trained-model release.
- Have teammates ask two questions each. Finish with a clear handoff or a short, honest answer.
