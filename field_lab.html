FIELD LAB — standalone campaign instrument

Self-contained microphone capture, PCM analysis, campaign log and CSV. No imports, build step, backend, upload or source classifier. Open the separate TonalWatch app.

Uncalibrated screening only. No source identification or legal-compliance determination.

Weighted levels are dB re digital RMS 1. C−A describes the captured spectrum. The low-frequency screen and sustained narrowband peak check are independent; neither establishes a source or legal tonality. Old stored rows remain visibly legacy/unverified.

Campaign protocol & experiments

  1. Record stationary when comparing stationary observations. Label walking captures separately. Enter what you heard and the conditions as user notes.
  2. Repeat captures to describe variability, retaining all observations and any exclusion reasons. A larger C−A gap alone does not establish a tone or require a source conclusion.
  3. Export the CSV before closing if browser storage is unavailable. The last captured PCM can also be downloaded as a float WAV for independent reanalysis.
E1 — duration comparison. Compare actual 5/15/30-second recordings, with all complete spectral windows included. Longer browser recordings do not establish compliance with a professional measurement procedure.
E2 — distance · E3 — masking · E4 — walking versus stationary. Enter estimated distance, surrounding sounds and movement in the notes. Changes in source activity, placement, microphone response and processing can change results; these experiments do not establish a general attenuation law.
E5 — recurring hum / spectrum observation. Describe the audible sound and capture its spectrum. Equipment names remain user hypotheses; a peak or a large gap does not identify a fan, transformer, building or transmitted sound.
T3 — 60/120 Hz neighborhoods, buddy comparison and passing events. Report only actual persistent local peak bins with their resolution. Buddy comparisons describe differences and are not calibration offsets. Tag passing events explicitly rather than silently deleting data.

Method, clock and storage limits

Version tw-field-lab-v2 uses sample-counted first-channel microphone PCM after 0.5 s warm-up. It applies a periodic Hann window (8,192 samples, 4,096-sample hop), removes each frame’s mean, averages linear one-sided power density across all complete frames, and integrates A/C responses normalized at 1 kHz over 20 Hz–20 kHz. Silence, clipping, non-finite samples, incomplete capture and unsupported rates make numeric indicators unavailable. Browser processing flags remain attached. This is not hardware or acoustic field validation.

The UTC capture timestamp is the browser-clock receipt of the first post-warm-up sample notification; clock accuracy is not established. Sample count, effective rate and analyzed duration are stored separately. Old log records are retained unchanged inside a legacy wrapper and exported in the legacy_original_json column; old numeric values and labels are not revalidated. Invalid stored JSON is not overwritten until the user explicitly clears it.

Recording a peak near 60/120 Hz does not identify a source; not finding one does not rule out a sound or establish microphone sensitivity. C−A changes with spectrum and device processing, not only source changes. Constant gain cancels only for otherwise identical unclipped samples; microphone response shaping does not.

Audio and campaign entries remain on this device. CSV/WAV downloads require a deliberate click. Keep synthetic demonstrations, externally reported data and real recordings distinct. Uncalibrated screening only; no source identification or legal-compliance determination.
