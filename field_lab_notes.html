Make a descriptive observation

  1. Open Observation and choose Microphone, File or an explicitly synthetic demo. Keep the original input kind with the result.
  2. For a microphone recording, use a duration offered by the app and remain in place. For file import, follow the app’s stated crop policy. Record the actual analyzed duration and effective sample rate shown in the result and report.
  3. Review recording-quality flags before interpreting numbers. Unavailable or inconclusive values remain unavailable; they are not zero.
  4. Add location, conditions, what you heard and any recurrence as user observations. A suspected source remains an unverified note.
  5. Review the report and print/save PDF or download its text packet. Keep that packet with any hand-entered log entry so its method, spectrum, quality and provenance remain inspectable.

Keep the indicators separate

Captured-spectrum C−A describes the difference between two weightings applied to the same digital recording. At or above the prototype’s 15 dB threshold, the wording is “low-frequency contribution suggested.”

The sustained narrowband peak is a separate experimental check. A larger gap alone does not establish a tone or identify a fan, transformer, light fixture or building.

A peak near 60 or 120 Hz is a frequency observation. Several acoustic and recording-chain causes can produce it; its presence does not identify mains equipment, and its absence does not validate microphone sensitivity.

Weighted digital values are dB re digital RMS 1, uncalibrated input. They are not sound-pressure levels.

Describe comparisons honestly

A previous-capture comparison, when available, is the observed change in C−A between two analyses in the current browser session. Keep each recording’s input kind, duration, sample rate, time and processing limitations.

Sound, placement, microphone response and processing can change the difference. It is not a calibration offset and does not make devices interchangeable. Constant gain cancels only for otherwise identical, unclipped samples; response shaping does not.

Repeated captures can describe variability. Longer recordings, distance changes or walking do not establish a legal measurement method, a general attenuation law or a shared source.

Keep the original notes as evidence of what was written

The field log preserves the supplied numbers and wording as unverified hand-entered notes. They do not establish which instrument, sample window, processing state or quality checks produced each value.

The reported 0.46 dB “device offset” remains an unverified original claim. Its derivation is not documented by the displayed pairs. Do not apply it to correct readings or pool devices.

New entries should reference an exported app packet. Keep old values separate rather than relabeling them as validated measurements.

const A_CON = {f1:20.598997, f2:107.65265, f3:737.86223, f4:12194.217};
function RA(f){const {f1,f2:f1sq,f3,f4}=A_CON,f2v=f*f;return f4*f4*f2v*f2v/((f2v+f1*f1)*Math.sqrt((f2v+f1sq*f1sq)*(f2v+f3*f3))*(f2v+f4*f4));}
function RC(f){const {f1,f4}=A_CON,f2v=f*f;return f4*f4*f2v/((f2v+f1*f1)*(f2v+f4*f4));}
const gA=f=>RA(f)/RA(1000), gC=f=>RC(f)/RC(1000);

function draw(){

  g.fillStyle='#0b0f13'; g.fillRect(0,0,c.width,c.height); g.fillStyle='#7fd47f';
  const n=buf.length, nyq=ctx.sampleRate/2, binHz=nyq/n, w=c.width;
  const step=Math.max(1,Math.floor(n/w));
